## Measured (local reference, not a capacity claim)

**Traffic to a zoomed-out observer (2026-10-14, Go simulation, no network).** `go test -run ZoomedOutTraffic -v ./internal/world`: 100 people spread over the default office, movers turning every 1.5 s on average, one observer in the middle, one simulated minute, WebSocket payload bytes. Everybody walking: 1,725 B/s (86 records/s) at the default radius, 2,433 B/s (135 records/s) at radius 8 (the whole office). A quarter walking: 772 B/s and 1,045 B/s. That is why a reduced update rate for far people was not built ([decision 0018](decisions/0018-physical-keys-and-map-zoom.md)). Frame headers, the WebSocket and TCP overhead, and real clients are not included.

Scenario A (no media) up to 1,000 bots and a 500-client reconnect storm, with the generator on the same laptop: see [Benchmark results](benchmark-results.md). Media through a real SFU (B, D and a scaled C, 0 % loss up to 40 people in calls, ~1.3-1.8 % of one M1 core per forwarded Mbit/s) is in the same document. Highlights: state-change records cut server output about 5x and brought the worst tick at 1,000 concentrated bots from 143 ms to 60 ms; a 500-client mass reconnect finished in 2.0 s with no failures.

**On a real host, generator on another machine (2026-10-01).** The production stack on the free Oracle VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro (São Paulo), loaded over the internet from one laptop in São Paulo: presence runs of 50 to 500 bots all joined with nothing kicked. 100 bots kept every tick percentile low (p99 5 ms); at 200 the mean tick was still 3 ms and the median move reached others in 88 ms, but p99 went above 50 ms and the worst tick took 180 ms; from 300 steal passed 70 %, consistent with the shape's CPU cap, and at 500 the mean tick (99 ms) exceeded the 66 ms period. Media: 4 people with video and 20 in audio-only groups ran clean (0 % and 0.5 % loss); 12 with video lost 27 %, 20 with video 63 %, and the 60-person audio-only run (59 connected) 51 % at only 4.8 Mbit/s. The VM's CPU cap is the most likely limit, but the generator laptop's CPU and upload were not measured. Single runs; tables in [Benchmark results](benchmark-results.md#on-a-free-cloud-vm-over-the-internet).

**Movement and rendering costs (2026-10-02, Apple M1 Pro, single machine).** `go test -bench Tick ./internal/world` with 300 players, a third of them changing direction every tick (far busier than people are): 1.99 -> 0.52 ms per tick concentrated in the reception and 1.12 -> 0.38 ms spread over the map, three runs each, same allocations; output bytes are unchanged. Chrome (ANGLE on Metal, 1440x900) with nobody moving, three 10 s windows per build: renderer process 10.2 -> 5.4 % and GPU process 15.4 -> 3.3 % of one core. Before = the previous commit, after = [decision 0017](decisions/0017-movement-reconciliation-and-render-on-demand.md). Neither number is a capacity claim; the free VM was not re-measured.

## Not run / not implemented

- **Scenario C at its full size** (100 people in 25 calls). B (20 people), a scaled C (40 people) and D (20-person meeting) were run locally with real synthetic media; 60 people and above saturate a single laptop. On the free cloud VM media saturates much earlier (see above). TURN through restrictive networks is untested.
- **The 2 vCPU / 4 GB reference server with dedicated CPUs.** Only the CPU-throttled free micro VM was measured with a separate generator. A two-hour soak (only a 10-minute, 300-bot presence soak was run: flat goroutines/RSS, see benchmark-results.md).
- **Browser FPS/CPU on the reference laptop.** Measured only on an Apple M1 Pro: 59.9 FPS (normal) and 29.6 FPS (economy, capped) with 300 bots, see [Benchmark results](benchmark-results.md).
- **The movement fix on the public demo.** The demo still runs the previous image; the fix was verified locally (with injected jitter), not over the internet on the demo host, and the VM benchmarks were not rerun with the faster tick.
- **TURN relay on a public host through UDP-blocked networks.** Direct UDP media and the production Compose stack with Caddy/TLS were verified on the demo; relay behaviour was not.
- **Moderation is minimal:** admins can remove members and revoke invites, but there is no ban list, temporary mute, reporting flow or content moderation; the audit log is not tamper-proof.
- **Accessibility:** only the automated axe-core audit and keyboard checks were run. The game canvas is not operable with a screen reader and nobody has tested with NVDA, VoiceOver or JAWS.
- **Cost numbers:** only the formula and `bench/cost.py` exist; no prices were verified.
- Only English is shipped (the i18n dictionary mechanism exists for more locales).
